The map: phase space

Plot position across and velocity up. One dot on this map is the complete state of the particle: where it is and how fast it is moving. As time passes the dot moves.

Under the exact laws of motion the dot goes round one closed loop for ever. Each grey loop is a line of constant energy, bigger loops mean more energy, and the true motion never changes the energy.

A real system of atoms has positions and velocities, so its map has axes instead of two. The picture is the same: one dot, moving on a surface of constant energy.

1Time-reversible

Film the true motion and play the film backwards: the reversed film also obeys Newton’s law. The test for an algorithm is the same. Run forward, flip the sign of the velocity, then run the same number of steps again. A time-reversible algorithm arrives back exactly where it started.

2Area-preserving

Start not one particle but a whole patch of slightly different starting states on the map. The exact motion can stretch and bend the patch, but it never changes its area. (For atoms the map has axes and “area” becomes “volume”; the statement is the same.) A good algorithm has to keep the area fixed too. A patch that keeps growing is the mark of an algorithm that pumps energy into the system. A patch that keeps shrinking is the mark of one that drains energy out.

What to look for. The coloured patch is where the algorithm puts the cloud of states. The dark outline is where the exact motion puts the same cloud. The number under each panel is the patch area divided by its starting area.

Then raise towards 1: Euler explodes at once, the RK4 patch visibly shrinks, and the velocity-Verlet patch gets out of step with the exact outline (a timing error) but keeps its area.

Substitute the first line into the other two and you get exactly equations (1) and (2) of section 4. It is the same algorithm, written in a different order.

Each move changes only one of the two coordinates, by an amount that depends only on the other one. On the map that is a slide. In a kick, every vertical column of states moves up or down as one piece. In a drift, every horizontal row moves sideways as one piece. Slide the cards of a deck and its side changes shape but not area. Three slides in a row still leave the area unchanged, for any and any force.

3No long-term energy drift

The exact motion keeps the total energy constant. No algorithm with a finite time step can do that perfectly. What matters is the kind of error. A small wobble around the right value is harmless. A steady creep is fatal for MD, because a long run then ends up at the wrong energy, which means the wrong temperature.

What to look for. Each algorithm runs for 100 time units from the same start. The charts show its energy divided by the starting energy, so the exact answer is a flat line at 1.

How the three properties fit together

Properties 1 and 2 are the cause; property 3 is the effect. Because velocity-Verlet is time-reversible and area-preserving, its dot cannot spiral in or out. It stays on a closed loop that is a slightly distorted copy of the true one, so its energy can only wobble.

RK4 is far more accurate per step, but it has neither property. Its small error always points the same way, so it adds up. In a real many-atom system tiny errors grow so fast that no algorithm can follow the exact path of each atom for long. What a simulation needs is to stay on the right energy surface over millions of steps. RK4 also needs four force calculations per step, and forces are the expensive part.

    Each algorithm below is run with its own formulas from the same start. The positions agree to the last digit the computer stores. That is not a coincidence or an approximation: the derivations in the notes show the three are the same recipe rearranged.

    What each one carries, and what that costs

    • Velocity-Verlet. Position and velocity at the same instant. Kinetic and potential energy are available together at every step. This is the LAMMPS default.
    • Verlet. Two positions and no velocity. The velocity is only available one step late, and the first step needs an extra starting position.
    • Leap-frog. Position, and a velocity half a step away from it. Kinetic and potential energy are never known at the same instant without the extra averaging line. This is the GROMACS default.
